Notebook 02

Probability distributions in practice

Four applied problems, each a different distribution. The defaults are the notebook's numbers; the results match its outputs.

P(Y = y) for the chosen period. The darker bars are the ones counted in P(Y ≥ m).

With many people and a small p, the binomial is close to a Poisson with λ = np. The chart compares the two.

Survival curves S(x): the chance of riding x thousand miles without a failure. A shape k above 1 means wear-out, so a used motorcycle is less reliable than a new one; the exponential has no memory.

Notebook 03

Monte Carlo simulation

When the answer is hard to derive, simulate it. Throw darts at random, drop accidents on a highway, and watch the estimate settle. Each run is seeded, so it can be repeated.

The dart game

A 12 in square board, rings of radius 2, 4 and 6 in scoring 25, 15 and 5

The running estimate of the expected score as throws accumulate, against the exact value.

Accidents on a 10-mile highway

One uniform location, two normal ones: how often are all three at least the minimum distance apart?

The estimate from ten seeds. When they agree to within 5 %, the simulation has converged, as the notebook checks.

Notebook 04

Estimation and hypothesis testing

Are the gas prices of two cities different? How precisely do we know a battery's remaining capacity? How many measurements does an astronomer need? Edit the data and the answers follow.

Gas prices in two cities

Welch's t-test for the means and an F-test for the variances

Significance level

Confidence intervals

For a mean (t) and a variance (χ²); defaults are the Tesla battery capacity sample

Confidence

How many measurements?

The sample size that puts the mean within a margin of error

n = (z σ / E)², rounded up. The notebook's astronomer, σ = 2 light-years and ±0.5, needs 62 measurements for 95 % and 107 for 99 %.

Tesla battery range

Histograms, scatter and box plots of a crowd-sourced survey of Tesla owners.

Key result: typical full-charge range about 240 mi (mean 241.8, median 238); 80 % of vehicles fall below 256 mi.

Probability distributions in practice

Café arrivals, pooled blood testing, shaft tolerances and motorcycle reliability.

Key result: about 10 % of shafts fall outside ±0.005 in; σ must drop to about 0.0019 in for ≤ 1 % defects.

Monte Carlo simulation and correlation

Airline accidents, battery capacity against mileage, a dart game and highway accidents.

Key result: capacity and mileage correlate at r = −0.58, whatever the mileage unit.

Estimation and hypothesis testing

Confidence intervals, sample-size planning and a comparison of gasoline prices.

Key result: no significant difference in mean gas price (Welch p = 0.24), but the variances differ strongly (ratio 16.8).
